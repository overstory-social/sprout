# Playing on the Playdate

The Playdate has no keyboard, so you do not type a command. You build it,
one choice at a time, by turning the crank. This chapter is for the person
holding the console. The app reaches a Playdate the way any game made
outside the catalogue does: as a `.pdx` folder, zipped and sideloaded from
your account at play.date, or sent over USB from the Playdate Simulator's
Device menu. Building that folder is the maintainer's job and is in the
repository's README.

## The shelf

When the app opens you see the shelf: the worlds on your Playdate, each a
word on a disc. Turn the crank, or press up and down, to turn it, and press
**A** to open the one in front. A world is named by its title. One shown in
grey cannot be opened; the panel at the bottom of the screen says why (it
may need a newer app than yours, or be too big for the Playdate's memory).

A fresh install has no worlds on it: the shelf holds only `more worlds...`,
which fetches some (see [More worlds](#more-worlds) below).

## Choosing a name

Before you go in you choose who you are there. The crank (or up and down)
turns through a list of plain names, such as Alder, Birch and Heron. Names
another person in that world already has, and names the world will not
allow, are left out. Press **A** to arrive. If you have been here before, the
name you used last is the first one offered. **B** takes you back to the
shelf.

## Reading

The world's words scroll up the screen as you play, newest at the bottom.
The top lines show where you are, who is with you and which ways lead out.
Turn the crank to read back over what was said; press **A** when you want to
do something.

The text comes in two sizes. Open the system menu (the menu button) and
set **text** to S or L; the app remembers it.

## The crank sentence builder

Pressing **A** starts a sentence. The top of the screen shows what you have
so far, and the choices turn on a disc below it; the one lying level at the
front is the one you take. Turn the crank (or press up and down) to turn the
disc, and press **A** to take the choice. The sentence goes in the same order
each time:

1. **What to do**: `take`, `ask`, `go`, and the rest of what the world has
   taught you here.
2. **What to do it to**, once for each thing the action needs: a thing in the
   room, a thing you carry, or a way out. Where the action can take several
   things at once, `everything` is offered first; where it can take an extra
   thing it does not need, the word that would introduce it (`from`, `with`)
   is offered beside `that's all`.
3. **A word or a number**, when the action needs one: `ask the apprentice
   about` `the press`. A number is on the crank too; left and right move it
   by ten.
4. **Done**: press **A** once more to do it.

**B** steps back one choice, and out of the sentence when you are at the
start. If you are building an action that takes several things at once, after
the first the list offers the others, and `that's all` ends the list.

A choice shown in grey cannot be done right now. Its reason is written under
it, such as "It is locked.", so you do not have to try it to find out.

Try it in `Printers Shop`: arrive, press **A**, turn to `ask`, **A**, `the
apprentice`, **A**, `the press`, **A**, and **A** again to do it.

## Pictures

Some worlds show a picture in a room or when something happens. It fills
the screen, with its caption under it, before you read on. When a move shows
more than one, turn the crank to step between them; **A** or **B** closes
them and the words come back.

## Saves and time

Your place in a world is saved for you after every move. You never save, and
there is nothing to lose by closing the app, switching the Playdate off or
letting its battery run down. The system menu (the menu button) has
**leave world**, which takes you back to the shelf, and **start over**, which
asks first — "Start this world over?" — and then forgets everything you
did there.

The world goes on while you read. Every ten seconds a moment passes, so a
tap that drips, a creature that wanders, or a guard who leaves his post does
so while the app is open. When you are away the world does not run, and when
you come back it catches up quietly: you arrive where you were, with no
account of what happened in between. If the Playdate's clock is set back, the
world never runs backwards.

If the Playdate has no room left for a save, the app says so, stops, and
greys the world on the shelf until a save works again, since a move that is
not kept would be lost anyway. If a save cannot be read at all, the app sets
it aside and starts the world again, and tells you.

## More worlds

The last word on the shelf is `more worlds...`. Press **A** to ask the
Playdate to fetch a list of worlds over Wi-Fi. The first time, the Playdate
asks whether the app may reach that server, and says what for: "Sprout
fetches the list of worlds and the ones you choose from this server." Say
yes. Pick a world with the crank and press **A** to download it; the panel
under the disc says who made it, its version and its size. The list is
signed, so the app refuses one that has been changed on the way, and each
world is checked before it joins your shelf. Where the list carries more
than one build of a world, only the newest is offered; a world already on
your shelf is shown in grey. If there is no Wi-Fi set up, you say no, or the
server cannot be reached, the screen says so, **B** goes back, and your shelf
is as it was. Worlds you download stay on the shelf, and so do their saves.
